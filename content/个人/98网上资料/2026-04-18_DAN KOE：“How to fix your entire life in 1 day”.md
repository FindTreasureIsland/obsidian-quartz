## How to fix your entire life in 1 day如何在一天内彻底改变你的人生

If you're anything like me, you think new years resolutions are stupid.如果你和我一样，你就会觉得新年决心很愚蠢。

Because most people go about changing their lives in the completely wrong way. They create these resolutions because everyone else does – we create a superficial meaning out of status games – but they don’t meet the requirements for true change, which goes a lot deeper than convincing yourself you’re going to be more disciplined or productive this year.因为大多数人改变生活的方式完全错误。他们制定这些计划只是因为其他人都这么做——我们把这种追求地位的游戏赋予了肤浅的意义——但这些计划并不符合真正改变的要求，真正的改变远比说服自己今年会更加自律或高效要深刻得多。

If you're one of these people, I'm not here to talk down on you (I tend to be a bit harsh in my writing). I’ve quit 10x more goals than I’ve achieved. I think that should be the case for most people. But the fact that people try to change their lives and utterly fail almost every time holds true.如果你属于这类人，我并非有意贬低你（我的文笔有时确实比较尖锐）。我放弃的目标比实现的要多十倍。我想大多数人都是如此。但人们尝试改变生活却几乎每次都以彻底失败告终，这的确是事实。

However, as much as I think new years resolutions are stupid, it’s always wise to reflect on the life you hate so you can launch yourself toward something that much better, as we will discuss.然而，尽管我认为新年决心很愚蠢，但反思自己讨厌的生活总是明智的，这样你才能朝着更好的方向前进，我们将在下文中讨论这一点。

So whether you want to start the business, transform your body, or take the risk toward a more meaningful life without quitting after 2 weeks, I want to share 7 ideas you probably haven’t heard before on behavior change, psychology, and productivity so you can do just that in 2026.所以，无论你是想创业、改变体型，还是想冒险追求更有意义的生活而不至于两周后就放弃，我都想分享 7 个你可能从未听说过的关于行为改变、心理学和生产力的想法，以便你在 2026 年能够做到这一点。

This will be comprehensive.这将是一项全面的工作。

This isn’t one of those letters that you read through and forget about.这不是那种读一遍就忘的信。

This is something you will want to bookmark, take notes on, and set aside time to think about.这是你需要收藏、记笔记并留出时间思考的内容。

The protocol at the end (to dig deep into your psyche and uncover what you truly want in life) will take about a full day to complete, with effects that last far longer than that.最后一步（深入挖掘你的内心世界，发现你真正想要的生活）大约需要一整天的时间才能完成，其效果持续时间要长得多。

Let’s begin.我们开始吧。

## I – You aren’t where you want to be because you aren’t the person who would be there我——你之所以没能到达你想去的地方，是因为你不是那个会待在那里的人。

When it comes to setting big goals, people tend to focus on one of the two requirements for success:在设定远大目标时，人们往往会关注成功的两个必要条件之一：

1. Changing your actions to make progress toward the goal (least important, second order)改变你的行动以朝着目标取得进展（最不重要，次要因素）
2. Changing who you are so that your behavior naturally follows (most important, first order)改变你的本质，让你的行为自然而然地随之改变（最重要，首要步骤）

Most people set a surface-level goal, hype themselves up to remain disciplined for the first few weeks, then go back to their old ways without much struggle, because they were trying to build a great life on a rotting foundation.大多数人设定一个肤浅的目标，给自己打气，让自己在前几周保持自律，然后就毫不费力地回到老样子，因为他们试图在一个腐朽的根基上建立美好的生活。

If this doesn’t make sense, let’s run through an example.如果这还不够清楚，我们来看一个例子。

Think of somebody successful. It can be a bodybuilder with a great physique, a founder/CEO worth hundreds of millions, or a charismatic dude who can chat up a group without a shred of anxiety entering his mind.想想那些成功人士。他可以是身材健硕的健美运动员，身价数亿美元的创始人/CEO，也可以是魅力四射、能与一群人侃侃而谈而丝毫不会感到焦虑的人。

Do you think the bodybuilder has to “grind” to eat healthy? Does the CEO have to discipline themselves to show up and lead the team? To you, it may seem like that on the surface, but the truth is that they can’t see themselves living any other way. The bodybuilder has to grind to eat **unhealthily**. The CEO has to force themself to lie in bed past their alarm clock, and they hate every second of it (there is nuance here, just entertain me for a second).你觉得健美运动员必须“拼命”才能吃得健康吗？CEO 必须自律才能按时到岗领导团队吗？表面上看似乎如此，但事实是他们无法想象自己过其他的生活。 健美运动员必须拼命才能吃得 **不健康** 。CEO 必须强迫自己赖床超过闹钟，而且他们讨厌其中的每一秒（这里面有很多细微差别，请允许我解释一下）。

To some people, my own lifestyle seems a bit extreme and disciplined. To me, it’s natural, and I don’t say that to contrast it with any other kind of lifestyle. I simply enjoy living this way. When my mom tells me that I should take a break, go out, and have some fun... I hold my tongue from telling her, “If I weren’t having fun, why would I be doing what I’m doing?”有些人觉得我的生活方式有点极端，也太自律了。对我来说，这很自然，我这么说并不是要把它和其他生活方式对立起来。我只是单纯地享受这种生活。当妈妈劝我休息一下，出去玩玩的时候……我忍住没跟她说：“如果我不觉得开心，那我干嘛还要做这些？”

This next sentence may sound simple, but it is baffling how many people don't get it.接下来这句话听起来很简单，但令人费解的是，竟然有那么多人不明白。

If you want a specific outcome in life, you must have the lifestyle that creates that outcome long before you reach it.如果你想在生活中取得某种特定的成果，你必须在实现该成果之前很久就拥有能够创造该成果的生活方式 。

If someone says they want to lose 30 pounds, I often don’t believe them. Not because I don’t think they are capable, but because there are too many times when that same person says, “I can’t wait until I'm done losing weight so I can start to enjoy life again.” I hate to break it to you, but if you don’t adopt the lifestyle that led to you losing the weight, for life, and find a reason with a higher gravitational pull than the one tying you to your previous ways, then you will go straight back to where you started, and you can unhappily say that you wasted the resource you will never get back: time.如果有人说他们想减掉 30 磅，我通常不太相信。不是因为我觉得他们不行，而是因为太多时候，这些人会说：“我迫不及待地想减掉体重，这样我就可以重新享受生活了。” 我不得不告诉你，如果你不能把让你减肥的生活方式坚持下去，并且找到一个比让你继续旧习惯更有吸引力的理由 ，那么你最终会回到原点，而且你会遗憾地承认，你浪费了永远无法挽回的资源：时间。

When you truly change yourself, all of your habits that don’t move the needle toward your goal become disgusting, because you have a deep and profound awareness of what kind of life those actions compound into. You are okay with your current standards because you are not fully aware of what they are or what they lead to. We will discuss how to uncover this, but we need to build up to that.当你真正改变自己时，所有那些对实现目标毫无帮助的习惯都会变得令人厌恶，因为你深刻地意识到这些行为最终会把你的生活变成什么样子。你之所以对目前的生活方式感到满意，是因为你并不完全了解它们是什么，也不清楚它们会导致什么后果。我们会探讨如何揭开这些真相，但这需要循序渐进。

You say you want to change. You say you want to “become financially free” and “get healthy,” but your actions show otherwise for a reason. And it goes a lot deeper than you think.你说你想改变，你说你想“实现财务自由”和“拥有健康的身体”，但你的行动却恰恰相反，这其中必有缘由。而且，这背后的原因远比你想象的要深远。

## II – You aren’t where you want to be because you don’t want to be there二、你之所以没能到达你想去的地方，是因为你根本不想待在那里。

> Trust only movement. Life happens at the level of events, not of words. Trust movement. – Alfred Adler只相信行动。生活发生在事件层面，而非言语层面。相信行动。 ——阿尔弗雷德·阿德勒

If you want to change who you are, you must understand how the mind works so that you can start to reprogram it.如果你想改变自己，就必须了解大脑的运作方式 ，这样才能开始重新编程它。

The first step to understanding the mind is to understand that all behavior is goal-oriented. It's teleological. When you think about it, this is kinda obvious, but when we dig into it, most people don’t want to hear it.理解心灵的第一步是理解所有行为都是目标导向的，即目的论。仔细想想，这似乎显而易见，但当我们深入探讨时，大多数人却不愿接受。

You take a step forward because you want to reach a certain location.你向前迈一步，是因为你想到达某个地方。

You scratch your nose because you want to make the itch go away.你挠鼻子是因为你想止痒。

Those ones are clear, but most of the time, your goals are unconscious. You may not realize that when you sit on the couch in the middle of the day, you are trying to burn time before your next responsibility, as one simple example.这些目标很明确，但大多数时候，你的目标是无意识的。举个简单的例子，你可能没有意识到，当你中午坐在沙发上时，你其实是在为下一个任务做准备，试图消磨时间。

On an even more unconscious and complex level, you pursue goals that can harm you, but you justify your actions in a way that is socially acceptable and doesn’t make you seem like a loser.在更深层次的无意识和复杂的层面上，你会追求可能伤害自己的目标，但你会用一种社会可接受的方式来为自己的行为辩解，这样你就不会显得自己像个失败者。

As an example, if you can’t stop procrastinating your work, you may justify it with the fact that you “lack discipline,” but in reality, you are attempting to achieve a goal like you always are. In this case, that goal could be to protect yourself from the judgment that comes from finishing and sharing your work.举例来说，如果你总是拖延工作，你可能会用“缺乏自律”来为自己辩解，但实际上，你和以往一样，都是在试图达成某个目标。在这种情况下，你的目标可能是为了避免完成并分享作品后所受到的评判。

If you say you want to quit your dead-end job, but stay in it without any real reason, you may start to think you don’t have enough courage, or that you were never really a “risk taker,” but the truth is that you are pursuing the goal of safety, predictability, and an excuse to not look like a failure to everyone else in your life who sees working a dead-end job as a sign of success.如果你说你想辞掉那份没有前途的工作，但却没有任何真正的理由继续待在那里，你可能会开始认为自己没有足够的勇气，或者你从来就不是一个“冒险者”，但事实是，你追求的是安全感、可预测性，以及一个不让自己在生活中其他人眼中显得失败的借口，因为在他们看来，从事一份没有前途的工作是成功的标志。

The lesson here is that real change requires changing your goals.这说明，真正的改变需要改变你的目标。

I don’t mean setting some surface-level goal because the act of doing that serves an unconscious goal that is actually harming you. That’s been ran through enough in the productivity space. I mean changing your point of view. Because that’s what a goal is. A goal is a projection into the future that acts as a lens of perception which allows you to notice information, ideas, and resources that aid in you achieving that goal.我指的不是设定一些肤浅的目标， 因为这样做会助长一种潜意识里对你有害的欲望 。这一点在效率提升领域已经被讨论得够多了。我指的是改变你的视角。 因为目标本质上就是改变你的视角。目标是对未来的一种展望，它就像一面透镜，让你能够注意到那些有助于你实现目标的信息、想法和资源。

Now let’s dig a bit deeper, because if you don’t understand this, it only becomes more difficult to get out.现在让我们深入探讨一下，因为如果你不理解这一点，只会让脱身变得更加困难。

## III – You aren’t where you want to be because you’re afraid to be there第三点——你之所以没能到达你想去的地方，是因为你害怕到达那里。

> The important thing for you to remember is that it does not matter in the least how you got the idea or where it came from. You may never have met a professional hypnotist. You may never have been formally hypnotized. But if you have accepted an idea - from yourself, your teachers, your parents, friends, advertisements, from any other source - and further, if you are firmly convinced that idea is true, it has the same power over you as the hypnotist’s words have over the hypnotized subject. – Maxwell Maltz你最需要记住的是，你如何获得这个想法或它来自哪里都无关紧要。你可能从未见过专业的催眠师，也可能从未接受过正式的催眠。但是，如果你接受了某种想法——无论是来自你自己、你的老师、你的父母、朋友、广告，还是任何其他来源——并且你坚信这个想法是正确的，那么它对你的影响就如同催眠师的话语对被催眠者的影响一样。 ——麦克斯韦·马尔茨

Here’s how you’ve become who you are today, and how you will become who you will be tomorrow. This is the anatomy of identity:这就是你如何成为今天的你，以及你如何成为明天的你的原因。这就是身份的构成：

1. You want to achieve a goal你想实现一个目标
2. You perceive reality through the lens of that goal你透过那个目标的视角来看待现实。
3. You only notice “important” information and ideas that allows you to achieve that goal (learning)你只会注意到那些能帮助你实现学习目标的“重要”信息和想法。
4. You act toward that goal and receive feedback that you are progressing toward it你朝着那个目标采取行动，并收到反馈，表明你正在朝着目标前进。
5. You repeat that behavior until it becomes automatic and unconscious (conditioning)你重复这种行为，直到它变成自动的、无意识的（条件反射）。
6. That behavior becomes a part of who you think you are (”I am the type of person who...”)这种行为会成为你对自己认知的一部分（“我是那种人……”）。
7. You defend your identity to maintain psychological consistency你捍卫自己的身份是为了维持心理一致性。
8. Your identity shapes new goals, restarting the cycle, and if that identity is disadvantageous toward a good life, this gets bad very quick你的身份认同会塑造新的目标，从而重新开始循环；如果这种身份认同不利于美好生活，情况就会迅速恶化。

The unfortunate reality is that you must break the cycle between steps 6 and 7, but this process starts when you are a child.令人遗憾的是，你必须打破第 6 步和第 7 步之间的循环，但这个过程要从你还是个孩子的时候就开始了。

You have the goal of survival.你的目标是生存。

You are dependent on your parents to teach you how to survive. You had to conform. And since the way most people teach is through reward and punishment, unless you adopt their beliefs and values, you will be punished. You don’t actually think for yourself until you see through this.你依赖父母教你如何生存。你不得不顺从。而大多数人的教育方式是奖惩分明，所以除非你接受他们的信仰和价值观，否则你就会受到惩罚。只有当你看穿这一切，你才能真正独立思考。

But your parents have also gone through this process throughout their entire lives. That’s where it can get dangerous. Your parents, unless they broke the pattern themselves, were conditioned by the culturally accepted ideas of success from the Industrial age. They also carry the best and worst conditioning from their parents and their parents’ parents.但你的父母一生中也经历了同样的过程。这正是危险所在。除非他们自己打破了这种模式，否则你的父母都会受到工业时代文化中普遍接受的成功观念的影响。他们也继承了父母和祖父母身上最好和最坏的观念。

To take it a layer deeper, once you fulfill your physical survival needs (which is quite easy to do in today’s world, you’re practically born into safety), you start to survive on the conceptual or ideological level. You may not try to protect and reproduce your body, but you absolutely protect and reproduce your mind. It’s not difficult to see the war of ideas on the internet, and the participants are individual and group identities.更深入地来说，一旦你满足了生理生存需求（在当今世界这很容易做到，你几乎生来就享有安全），你便开始在概念或意识形态层面生存。你或许不会试图保护和繁衍你的身体，但你绝对会保护和繁衍你的思想。不难看出互联网上的思想之战，而参与者既有个人身份，也有群体身份。

When your body feels threatened, you go into fight or flight.当你的身体感到受到威胁时，你会进入战斗或逃跑状态。

When your identity feels threatened, the same thing happens.当你的身份认同受到威胁时，同样的事情也会发生。

If you are heavily identified with a political ideology (by the process we talked about just before), you will feel threatened when someone challenges your beliefs. You literally feel the stress. You feel, emotionally, like you were just slapped in the face. Since most people don’t analyze their emotions for truth, you tend to get stuck in echo chambers and double down on claims that harm yourself and others.如果你对某种政治意识形态认同过深（就像我们刚才讨论的那样），当有人挑战你的信仰时，你会感到威胁。你会真切地感受到压力，就像被人狠狠扇了一巴掌。由于大多数人不会分析自己的情绪以求真相，你往往会陷入回音室效应，并更加固执己见，最终伤害自己和他人。

If you were raised in a religious household, and did not think for yourself, you will fight and attack others who threaten your psychological safety within that little bubble.如果你在宗教家庭长大，没有独立思考的能力，那么当有人威胁到你在那个小圈子里的心理安全时，你就会攻击和反抗。

The same thing happens when you unconsciously see yourself as a lawyer, a gamer, or somebody else who would not take the actions to achieve a better life.当你无意识地将自己视为律师、游戏玩家或其他不会采取行动去追求更好生活的人时，也会发生同样的情况。

## IV – The life you want lies within a specific level of mind第四点——你想要的生活存在于某种特定的心智层次之中。

The mind evolves through predictable stages over time.人的思维会随着时间的推移经历可预测的阶段而发展。

When you’re born, you’re like a little survival sponge that absorbs whatever beliefs you can (which are heavily dictated by your culture) so that you can feel safe and secure. And if you don’t be careful, your mind may crystalize and it may make it difficult to live a meaningful life.你出生时就像一块小小的求生海绵，吸收着你能接触到的一切信念（这些信念很大程度上受你的文化影响），从而获得安全感。如果你不加注意，你的思维可能会变得僵化，让你难以过上有意义的生活。

This has been documented enough in models like Maslow’s Hierarchy, Greuter’s stages of ego development, Spiral Dynamics, and Integral Theory, each building off of one another, but it’s also not difficult to observe in society.这一点在马斯洛的需求层次理论、格鲁特的自我发展阶段理论、螺旋动力学理论和整体理论等模型中都有充分的记载，这些模型彼此相互借鉴，而且在社会中也很容易观察到这一点。

I’ve synthesized these models into the 80/20 of the 9 stages of ego development as a refresher (repetition helps reveal things you didn’t notice before, and there are new people reading these letters):

1. **Impulsive** — No separation between impulse and action. Black and white thinking. I.e. A toddler hits when angry because the feeling and the behavior are the same thing.**冲动型** ——冲动与行动之间没有界限。非黑即白的思维模式。 例如，幼儿生气时会打人，因为在他们看来，生气的感觉和打人的行为是一回事。
2. **Self-Protective** — The world is dangerous and you learn to look out for yourself. I.e. A kid learns to hide report cards, lie about chores, and figure out what adults want to hear.**自我保护** ——世界充满危险，你必须学会保护自己。 例如，孩子会学会藏起成绩单、谎报家务，以及琢磨大人想听什么。
3. **Conformist** — You are your group and its rules feel like reality itself. I.e. Someone who genuinely cannot fathom why anyone would vote differently than their family or group.**墨守成规者** ——你把自己等同于你的群体，并将群体规则视为现实本身。 例如，你真的无法理解为什么有人会投票给与自己家人或群体不同的人。
4. **Self-Aware** — You notice you have an inner life that doesn’t match the exterior. I.e. Sitting in church and realizing you’re not sure you believe what everyone around you seems to believe, but not knowing what to do with that feeling yet.**自我觉察** ——你发现自己的内心世界与外在表现并不一致。 例如，坐在教堂里，你意识到自己并不确定是否相信周围所有人似乎都相信的事情，但你却不知道该如何处理这种感觉。
5. **Conscientious** — You build your own system of principles and hold yourself accountable to them. I.e. Leaving your family’s religion after careful study and adopting a personal philosophy you can defend, or building a career plan with clear milestones because you believe the right effort yields the right results.**认真负责** ——你会建立自己的一套原则体系，并严格遵守这些原则。 例如，经过仔细研究后离开家族宗教，并采纳一套自己能够捍卫的个人哲学；或者制定包含明确里程碑的职业规划，因为你相信付出正确的努力就能获得正确的结果。
6. **Individualist** — You see that your principles were shaped by context and start holding them more loosely. I.e. Realizing your political views have more to do with where you grew up than objective truth, or noticing that your ambitious career goals were really about earning your father’s approval.**个人主义者** ——你意识到自己的原则是受环境影响而形成的，于是开始更加灵活地看待它们。 例如，你意识到自己的政治观点更多地与你的成长环境有关，而非客观真理；或者你发现自己雄心勃勃的职业目标实际上是为了赢得父亲的认可。
7. **Strategist** — You work with systems while aware of your own involvement in them. I.e. Leading an organization while actively questioning your own blind spots, or engaging in politics knowing your perspective is partial and shaped by bias you can’t fully see.**战略家** ——你运用各种系统，同时意识到自身在其中的角色。 例如，领导一个组织时，你会积极反思自身的盲点；或者参与政治活动时，你会意识到自己的视角是片面的，并且受到一些你无法完全看清的偏见的影响。
8. **Construct-Aware** — You see all frameworks, including your identity, as useful fictions. I.e. Holding your spiritual beliefs with metaphorically not literally, knowing the map is not the territory, or watching yourself play the role of “founder” or “thought leader” with a kind of gentle amusement.**建构意识** ——你将所有框架，包括你的身份认同，都视为有用的虚构。 例如，你以比喻而非字面意义来持有你的精神信仰，明白地图并不等同于疆域本身，或者以一种温和的趣味看待自己扮演“创始人”或“思想领袖”的角色。
9. **Unitive** — Separation between self and life dissolves. I.e. Work, rest, and play feel like the same thing. There’s no one left who needs to become something, just presence responding to what arises.**合一型** ——自我与生活之间的界限消融。 工作、休息和娱乐成为同一件事。没有人还需要成为什么，一切都只是临在回应当下发生的一切。

For most people reading this, I would assume you hover between 4 and 8, which is a huge gap. Those closer to 8 are reading this are doing so to either learn something or pass time in a non-destructive way. Those closer to 4 are really looking for a change. You feel like you are meant for more, but you can’t make sense of everything yet, because there’s obviously a lot at play.对于大多数读到这篇文章的人来说，我估计你的状态介于第4阶段和第8阶段之间，这是一个巨大的跨度。接近第8阶段的人读这些内容，要么是为了学点东西，要么只是为了以一种无害的方式消磨时间。接近第4阶段的人则真正渴望改变。你觉得自己注定要做更大的事情，但你还没有理清头绪，因为显然有太多因素在起作用。

The good thing is, it doesn’t really matter what stage you are in, because moving through any of them follows a pattern.好消息是，你在哪个阶段并不重要，因为穿越任何一个阶段都遵循同样的规律。

## V – Intelligence is the ability to get what you want out of life第五点——智力的本质是能够从生活中得到你想要的东西

> The only real test of intelligence is if you get what you want out of life. – Naval Ravikant智力真正的检验标准，是你能否从生活中得到你想要的东西。 ——尼维·拉维坎特

There is a formula for success.成功有一个公式。

One ingredient is **agency**.其中一个要素是**能动性**。

One ingredient is **opportunity** (which many people like to mistake as “privilege” - because they the other ingredients).另一个要素是**机会**（许多人喜欢把它误认为”特权”——因为他们没有其他要素）。

The last ingredient is **intelligence**.最后一个要素是**智力**。

If you have high agency but low opportunity, it doesn’t matter how likely you are to act toward a goal, because it isn’t a goal that will bear much fruit.如果你有高度的能动性但缺乏机会，无论你多么倾向于朝着目标行动都无济于事，因为它不是一个能结出丰硕果实的目标。

If you have opportunity and agency but low intelligence, then you will never be fully able to benefit from that opportunity.如果你有机会和能动性但缺乏智力，那你永远无法充分利用这些机会。

First, we’ve talked about agency before here. In terms of opportunity, I can’t tell you to change your physical location, but if you don’t see the abundance of digital opportunity right in front of you, I don’t know what to tell you.首先，我们之前已经讨论过能动性。在机会方面，我没法让你改变物理位置，但如果你看不到眼前丰富多样的数字机会，那我就无话可说了。

With that said, I want to focus on what intelligence is in the context of these two other ingredients and this letter. For that, we look to cybernetics.话虽如此，我想专注于在这两个要素和这封信的语境下探讨智力的本质。为此，我们需要借助控制论。

> Cybernetics comes from the greek word kybernetikos which means “to steer” or “good at steering.”控制论源自希腊语”kybernetikos”，意为”掌舵”或”善于掌控”。

It’s also known as “the art of getting what you want.”它也被称为”获得你所想要之物的艺术”。

So, if Naval’s definition of intelligence is getting what you want out of life, understanding cybernetics helps you do that much faster.因此，如果尼维对智力的定义是从生活中得到你想要的东西，那么理解控制论能帮助你更快地做到这一点。

Cybernetics illustrates the properties of intelligent systems.控制论阐明了智能系统的属性。

- To have a goal.有目标。
- Act toward that goal.朝着目标行动。
- Sense where you are.感知自己所处的位置。
- Compare it to the goal.将其与目标进行比较。
- And act again based on that feedback.并根据反馈再次行动。

You can judge intelligence based on the system’s ability to iterate and persist with trial and error.你可以根据系统通过试错进行迭代和坚持的能力来评判其智能水平。

A ship blown off course that corrects toward its destination. A thermostat sensing a change in heat and turning on. The pancreas excreting insulin after blood glucose spikes.一艘偏离航线的船会自行修正航向。恒温器感知温度变化并启动。胰脏在血糖飙升后分泌胰岛素。

What does this have to do with getting what you want out of life?这与从生活中得到你想要的东西有什么关系？

Everything.一切皆有关。

Acting, sensing, comparing, and understanding the system from a meta-perspective is fundamental to high intelligence (with the definition we are using here).行动、感知、比较，以及从元视角理解系统，是高智力的基础（这是我们这里使用的定义）。

High intelligence is the ability to iterate, persist, and understand the big picture. **The mark of low intelligence is the inability to learn from your mistakes.**高智力是迭代、坚持和理解全局的能力。**低智力的标志是无法从错误中吸取教训。**

Low-intelligence people get stuck on problems rather than solving them. They hit a roadblock and quit. Like a writer who fails to build a readership and quits because they lack the ability to try new things, experiment, and figure out a process that works for them (to think that there isn’t an effective process you can create is verifiably false, no matter your limiting beliefs, hence being low intelligence.)低智力的人会在问题面前停滞不前，而不是去解决它们。他们遇到障碍就放弃。就像一个作家无法建立起读者群就放弃写作一样，因为他们缺乏尝试新事物、进行实验并找出适合自己方法的能力（认为不存在一个可以创造出的有效方法，这种想法是经证实站不住脚的，不管你有什么限制性信念——这就是低智力的体现）。

High intelligence is realizing any problem can be solved on a large enough timescale. The reality is that you can achieve any goal you set your mind to.高智力在于认识到任何问题都可以在足够长的时间尺度上得到解决。事实是，你可以实现你下定决心要实现的任何目标。

Intelligence is realizing that there is a series of choices you can make which lead to achieving the goal you want. You understand that ideas are hierarchical and that you can’t go from papyrus to Google docs in one fell swoop. Even if that goal is impossible right now, you simply don’t have the resources – which may be invented over the next few years – to achieve that thing.智力在于认识到，有一系列的选择可以引导你实现你想要的目标。你理解想法是有层级的，你不可能从纸莎草纸一步跳到 Google 文档。即使那个目标现在不可能实现，你只是缺乏资源——而这些资源可能在未来几年内被发明出来。

When I talk about “goals,” and as I will continue repeating, I am not speaking from the typical lens of self-help, although that’s a helpful lens to adopt at times.当我谈论”目标”时——我还会继续重复这一点——我并不是从典型的自助类视角出发，尽管那有时是一个有用的视角。

I am speaking from the lens of teleology or the Greek kosmos – that everything serves a purpose. That everything is a part of a greater whole.我是从目的论或希腊语”kosmos”的视角出发——即万物皆有其目的，一切都是更大整体的一部分。

Goals determine how you see the world.目标决定了你看待世界的方式。

Goals determine what you consider “success” or “failure.”目标决定了什么对你来说是”成功”或”失败”。

You can try to “enjoy the journey,” but if you pursue the wrong goal, you will not enjoy it.你可以试着”享受过程”，但如果你追求的是错误的目标，你就不会乐在其中。

Your mind is the operating system for reality.你的心灵是现实的操作系统。

That system is composed of goals.这个系统由目标构成。

For most people, those goals are assigned to them. Programmed like lines of code in your psyche.对大多数人来说，这些目标是被赋予他们的。就像心理中的代码行一样被编程。

Go to school. Get the job. Get offended. Play victim. Retire at 65.上学。找工作。受冒犯。当受害者。65岁退休。

A known path that doesn’t work.一条走不通的已知路径。

To become more intelligent, you must:要变得更加聪明，你必须：

- Reject the known path拒绝已知路径
- Dive into the unknown潜入未知
- Set new, higher goals to expand your mind设定新的、更高的目标以扩展你的思维
- Embrace the chaos and allow for growth拥抱混乱，允许成长
- Study the generalized principles of nature研究自然的普遍原理
- Become a deep generalist成为一个深度通才

I understand this may not be the traditional definition of intelligence, but that sequence of steps leads to an extraordinary level of connections in your brain, leading to what we would observe as an intelligent person. Pair that with agency and you’ve got a winner.我知道这可能不是传统的智力定义，但这套步骤序列会在你的大脑中产生非凡的神经连接，进而产生我们所能观察到的聪明人。将之与能动性结合，你就成了赢家。

- **1:30pm:** If someone filmed the last two hours, what would they conclude I want from my life?- **下午1:30：** 如果有人拍摄了最后两个小时，他们会得出我想要从生活中得到什么的结论？
- **3:15pm:** Am I moving toward the life I hate or the life I want?- **下午3:15：** 我是在朝着我讨厌的生活前进，还是朝着我想要的生活前进？
- **5:00pm:** What’s the most important thing I’m pretending isn’t important?- **下午5点：** 我假装不重要的最重要的事是什么？
- **7:30pm:** What did I do today out of identity protection rather than genuine desire? (Hint: it’s most things you do)- **晚上7:30：** 今天我做的哪些事情是为了保护身份而非出于真正的渴望？（提示：你做的大部分事情都是如此）
- **9:00pm:** When did I feel most alive today? When did I feel most dead?- **晚上9点：** 今天什么时候我感觉最鲜活？什么时候我感觉最死气沉沉？

To add a bit more fuel to the fire, schedule these questions during times where you are either commuting, walking, or lying around.为了火上浇油，在通勤、散步或躺着的时候安排这些问题。

- What would change if I stopped needing people to see me as \[the identity you wrote in question 10\]?- 如果我不再需要别人把我看作\[你在问题10中写的身份\]，会有什么改变？
- Where in my life am I trading aliveness for safety?- 在我生活的什么地方，我正在用活力换取安全感？
- What’s the smallest version of the person I want to become that I could be tomorrow?- 我想成为的那个人，最小的版本是什么，我明天就可以成为？

**Part 3) Evening – Synthesizing Insight – Entering A Season Of Progress****第三部分）晚上——综合洞察——进入进步的季节**

If you followed that process, I would be surprised if you didn’t have at least one profound insight that could alter the course of your life. Now, we need to make those known, integrate them into who we are, and act on them to begin solidifying our journey to a new level of mind.如果你完成了这个过程，如果你没有获得至少一个可能改变人生轨迹的深刻洞察，我会感到惊讶。现在，我们需要让这些变得清晰，将它们整合到我们是谁之中，并采取行动，开始巩固我们走向新的心智层次的旅程。

1. After today, what feels most true about why you’ve been stuck?1. 今天之后，关于你为什么一直停滞不前，什么感觉最真实？
2. What is the actual enemy? Name it clearly. Not circumstances. Not other people. The internal pattern or belief that has been running the show.2. 真正的敌人是什么？清楚地命名它。不是环境。不是别人。是那个一直在幕后操纵的内在模式或信念。
3. Write a single sentence that captures what you refuse to let your life become. This is your anti-vision compressed. It should make you feel something when you read it.3. 写一句话，概括你拒绝让自己的生活变成什么。这是你的反愿景的压缩版。当你读它的时候，应该让你有所触动。
4. Write a single sentence that captures what you’re building toward, knowing it will evolve. This is your vision MVP.4. 写一句话，概括你正在朝着什么前进，知道它会不断演进。这是你的愿景MVP（最小可行版本）。

Lastly, we need to create goals.最后，我们需要创建目标。

Again, these aren’t goals that you set for the sake of achievement, because goals are just projections. They are unreliable and make you feel bound to something that will inevitably change. Instead, think of goals as a point of view. A lens that you can exchange to enter the right state of mind to perform the action that will lead away from the life you don’t want. Do not worry about some kind of finish line, because as we will find, it doesn’t exist. Enjoyment is found in progress.再说一遍，这些不是为成就而设定的目标，因为目标只是投射。它们不可靠，让你感觉被绑在某件必然会改变的事物上。相反，把目标看作一个观点。一种可以切换的透镜，让你进入正确的心智状态，执行能让你远离不想过的生活的行动。不要担心某种终点线，因为我们将会发现，它并不存在。乐趣存在于进步之中。

## VII – Turn Your Life Into A Video Game第七点——将你的人生变成一款视频游戏

> The optimal state of inner experience is one in which there is order in consciousness. This happens when psychic energy—or attention—is invested in realistic goals, and when skills match the opportunities for action. The pursuit of a goal brings order in awareness because a person must concentrate attention on the task at hand and momentarily forget everything else. – Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi最佳内心体验状态是意识中有秩序。这发生在心理能量——或注意力——被投入到现实目标中，而且技能与行动机会相匹配的时候。追求目标会在意识中带来秩序，因为一个人必须集中注意力于手头的任务，暂时忘记其他一切。 ——米哈里·契克森米哈

You now have all of the components that lead to a good life.你现在已经拥有了通向美好生活所需的所有要素。

Now, it may be helpful to organize all of your insights into one coherent plan. Pull out a new page and write down these 6 components:现在，把你所有的洞察组织成一个连贯的计划可能会有帮助。拿出一张新纸，写下这六个要素：

- **Anti-vision** – What is the bane of my existence, or the life I never want to experience again?**反愿景** ——我存在的克星是什么，或者说我绝不想再经历的生活是什么？
- **Vision** – What is the ideal life that I think I want and can improve as I work toward it?**愿景** ——我认为我想要的理想生活是什么，随着我朝着它努力可以不断改进？
- **1 year goal** – What will my life look like in 1 year time, and is that closer to the life I want?**一年目标** ——一年后我的生活会是什么样子，它是否更接近我想要的生活？
- **1 month project** – What do I need to learn? What skills do I need to acquire? What can I build that will move me closer to the one year goal?**一个月项目** ——我需要学习什么？我需要获得什么技能？我可以构建什么来让我更接近一年目标？
- **Daily levers** – What are the priority, needle-moving tasks that bring my project closer to completion?**每日杠杆** ——什么是优先级最高、推动项目进展的任务？
- **Constraints** – What am I not willing to sacrifice to achieve my vision from the ground up?**约束条件** ——为了从头开始实现我的愿景，我不愿意牺牲什么？

Why is this so powerful?为什么这如此强大？

Because these components literally create your own little world. If you are meant to pursue this hierarchy of goals at this stage of your life, you will have no other option but to become obsessed. You will feel the pull to something greater. You will not see anything else as an option.因为这些要素真正地创造了你自己的小世界。如果你注定要在这个人生阶段追求这个目标层次结构，你就会不由自主地着迷。你会感受到对更伟大事物的拉力。你不会把其他任何东西当作选项。

You turn your life into a video game.你将你的人生变成了一款视频游戏。

Because games are the poster child for obsession, enjoyment, and flow states. They have all the components that lead to focus and clarity, so if we reverse engineer what those components are, we can live in a state of deeper enjoyment, less distractions, and more success.因为游戏是”着迷”、”享受”和”心流状态”的典型代表。它们具备所有导致专注和清晰的因素，所以如果我们逆向工程这些因素是什么，我们就可以生活在更深层次的享受、更少的干扰和更多成功的状态中。

Your vision is how you **win**. At least until the game evolves.你的愿景是你如何**赢**。至少在游戏升级之前是这样。

Your anti-vision is what’s at **stake**. What happens if you lose or give up.你的反愿景是**赌注**。如果你输了或放弃了，会发生什么。

Your 1 year goal is the **mission**. This is your sole priority in life.你的一年目标是**使命**。这是你人生中的唯一优先项。

Your 1 month project is the **boss fight**. How you gain XP and acquire loot.你一个月项目是**Boss战**。你如何获得经验值和战利品。

Your daily levers are the **quests**. The daily process that unlocks new opportunities.你的每日杠杆是**任务**。解锁新机会的日常过程。

Your constraints are the **rules**. The limitations that encourage creativity.你的约束条件是**规则**。鼓励创造力的限制。

All of these act as a concentric set of circles, like a forcefield, that guard your mind from distractions and shiny objects.所有这些就像一组同心圆，像一道力场，保护你的心灵免受干扰和花哨事物的侵害。

The more you play the game, the stronger this force becomes, and soon enough it becomes who you are, and you wouldn’t have it any other way.你玩这个游戏越多，这种力量就越强大，很快它就成为了你是谁，而你不会想要其他方式。
